# Hanlish
Drawing on the conciseness of Chinese, English is optimized as follows (without suggesting retention of the current state):

# 1. Numbers  
The English words from one to ten remain unchanged, but:  
ten one = 11  
ten two = 12  
and so on.  
two ten = 20  
three ten = 30  
four ten = 40  
and so on.  
tenthousand = 10,000  

For ordinal numbers, simply add "th" after the numeral:  
oneth = 1st  
twoth = 2nd  
ten twoth = 12th  
and so on.  

# 2. Time & Calendar  
month one = January  
month two = February  
and so on.  
month ten one = November  
month ten two = December  

month 3 day 2 = March 2nd  

week one = Monday  
week two = Tuesday  
week three = Wednesday  
week four = Thursday  
week five = Friday  
week six = Saturday  
week seven = Sunday  

# 3. Tenses  
Past tense: uniformly add the suffix "ed".  
Perfect tense: "have" + past tense of the verb.  
Progressive tense: uniformly add the suffix "ing".  
eated breakfast = ate breakfast  
have eated breakfast = have eaten breakfast  
eating breakfast = be eating breakfast  

The verb "be" is used for all tenses, eliminating "am/are/is" and their tense forms (i.e., "was/were").

# 4. Third Person Singular  
Verbs following the third person singular remain unchanged.  
I eat; he/she eat  

# 5. Articles  
All articles are removed.  
one apple = an apple  

# 6. Plurals  
All countable nouns form the plural by simply adding the suffix "s". Nouns with a quantity of no more than one do not take the "s".

# 7. Elision  
All elided contractions are prohibited; e.g., "want to" cannot be abbreviated as "wanna".

# 8. Liaison  
All forms of liaison (connected speech) are prohibited; e.g., "I would have" cannot be contracted to "I'd've".

# 9. Prepositions  
When indicating time (e.g., in the morning/evening/night, etc.), dates, or days of the week, all prepositions are removed.  
Prepositions related to clothing are all removed. "Put on" is uniformly expressed as "on", and "take off" as "off".  
For boarding all forms of transportation, use "up"; for alighting, use "down".

# 10. Genitive Apostrophe  
For all instances of "A of B", uniformly use "'s" to indicate possession, regardless of whether A is animate or inanimate.  
e.g., John's leg, table's leg  

# 11. Subjective and Objective Pronouns  
All objective case pronouns (i.e., me, him, her, etc.) are removed, and subjective case pronouns are used uniformly.  
Possessive pronouns (without distinguishing between possessive adjectives and possessive pronouns) are uniformly formed using the subjective case + "'s".
